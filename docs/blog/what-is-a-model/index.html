What is a model?

Lesson 1 ended with this: AI is software whose rules were learned from examples. A model is the thing those rules end up stored in. It's the file — usually a very large file — holding billions of numbers that together act as the learned rules.

When someone says "the model" they mean that brain. When they say a model's name — GPT, Claude, Gemini, Llama — they're naming one particular brain, trained by one company, with its own learned rules.

The surprising job it learned

Here's the part that surprises everyone the first time they hear it. The model behind a chatbot was not trained to "be helpful" or "answer questions." It was trained on one tiny task:

Given the start of some text, predict the next word.

"The cat sat on the ___" — the model learned that "mat" is a good guess, "floor" is plausible, "quantum" is very unlikely. That's it. That's the whole skill it started with.

The catch is that doing this task extremely well on enormous amounts of text forces the model to learn real things underneath: grammar, facts, how code works, how a conversation is supposed to flow. Predicting the next word in "the capital of Japan is ___" requires knowing the capital of Japan. To guess the next line of a Python program, you have to understand the program.

So "next word prediction" is the training task, but understanding is what it took to get good at it.

Where the examples came from

Models are trained on a huge slice of the public internet — articles, books, documentation, forums, code. That explains both of a model's two famous traits:

Why it's broad. It has seen more written text than any person could read in a thousand lifetimes, so it can follow almost any topic you bring it.

Why it's imperfect. The internet is a mix of the true, the outdated, the biased, and the wrong. A model that learned patterns from all of it has absorbed all of it. It repeats confident nonsense sometimes because confident nonsense is also a pattern in its training data.

Why answers come one word at a time

Now the earlier definition clicks into place for chatbots. When you ask a question, the model predicts the most likely next word, adds it to the text, predicts the next word of the longer text, adds it — repeating until the answer is done. You can literally watch it: that's why chatbot answers stream in word by word instead of appearing all at once. The text doesn't exist yet; it's being produced as it's predicted.

Recap in one breath. A model is a big file of learned numbers. It learned by practicing one task — predict the next word — on a giant library of human writing. Get that right at scale and understanding falls out of it as a side effect.

Why this matters for everything else

This mental model pays off immediately. "ChatGPT" is a model plus a wrapper that makes it conversational. "An AI agent" — the subject of later lessons — is a model plus a wrapper that gives it tools. The model is always the same ingredient; different products are different things wrapped around it.
